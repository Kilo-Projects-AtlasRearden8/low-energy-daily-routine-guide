Welcome

This guide is for days when starting a task feels impossible — especially with ADHD, chronic illness, burnout, or just a hard day. It was written by Asdale Dynamics, LLC, a small business making beginner-friendly guides and practical tools. You do not need to do everything here. Pick one idea, try it for a day or two, and drop it if it does not help.

  • One task at a time. Not three. One.
  • Small steps count. Brushing your teeth is a win.
  • Rest is part of the routine. Not something you earn.
  • A bad day does not break a streak. There is no streak.
  • This is not medical advice. Talk to a clinician about health concerns.

One Task at a Time

When your energy is low, a to-do list can feel like a wall. The trick is to look at only one brick.

Pick one task. Do it. Stop. That is enough for now.

  1. Write down one tiny task. Make it almost silly: wash one mug, put on socks, drink a glass of water.
  2. Do that one thing. Do not think about what comes after.
  3. Stop when it is done. Notice that you did it.
  4. Decide if you want one more. If not, rest. Both outcomes are correct.

ADHD-friendly tips: try body doubling (someone nearby working quietly), use a 5–10 minute timer instead of a schedule, and lower the bar until you can step over it.

Gentle Schedules

Rigid schedules break when energy dips. Gentle schedules bend. Times are anchors, not rules.

TimeBlockWhat it might look like
MorningWake up gentlyOpen curtains, drink water, stretch one minute
Mid-morningOne small taskOne item from the low-energy task list
MiddayRest breakSit, lie down, or do nothing for 10–20 minutes
AfternoonOne small taskAnother tiny task — or skip it
Late afternoonEnergy checkRate your energy 1–5. Adjust plans down, never up
EveningWind-downDim lights, one calming thing, prepare for sleep

Rules: never schedule more than 2–3 things per day; build in rest blocks first; use around times; if you miss a block, skip it; rewrite the schedule when it stops working for you.

Rest Without Guilt

Rest is not a reward for finishing your to-do list. Rest is part of being a person.

  • Lying down in the middle of the day because your body asked for it
  • Saying “I am not doing anything else today” without explaining why
  • Taking a nap and not counting it against yourself
  • Watching a show, reading, or staring out a window with no goal

How to rest without the guilt spiral: name it out loud (“I am resting now”), set a loose container (“20 minutes” or “until I feel ready”), do not bargain (“rest after the dishes”), and notice what actually restored you.

Energy Tracking

Rate your energy a few times a day on a 1–5 scale:

RatingWhat it means
5Several things; can start new ones
4One or two tasks with breaks
3One small task, then rest
2Basic self-care: water, food, restroom
1Rest. Doing nothing is the right choice

Averaging your ratings helps you match tasks to the energy you actually have. A “1” day is a rest day, not a failed day.

Low-Energy Task List

Tiny tasks for when you cannot think of anything to do. Pick one. Or pick none.

  • Self-care: drink water, wash your face, put on clean socks, step outside one minute, brush your teeth
  • Around the home: put one dish away, fold one piece of laundry, throw away one piece of trash, wipe one counter
  • Movement: stretch 30 seconds, walk to the end of the block and back, roll your shoulders
  • Rest: lie down 10 minutes, close your eyes for one song, do nothing for five minutes (this counts)

Write 5–10 tasks that feel easy for you on a low day. If a task ever feels too big, make it smaller: “wash dishes” becomes “rinse one spoon.”
