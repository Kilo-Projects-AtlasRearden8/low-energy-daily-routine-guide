# Gentle Schedules

Rigid schedules break when energy dips. Gentle schedules bend.

## The idea

A gentle schedule is a loose suggestion, not a contract. It gives you a rhythm without demanding perfection. If you miss a block, the day is not ruined — you just pick up where you are.

## A sample gentle day

Times are anchors, not rules. Move them, skip them, or ignore them.

| Time | Block | What it might look like |
|------|-------|-------------------------|
| Morning | Wake up gently | Open curtains, drink water, stretch for one minute |
| Mid-morning | One small task | One task from your low-energy list (see task list) |
| Midday | Rest break | Sit, lie down, or do nothing for 10–20 minutes |
| Afternoon | One small task | Another tiny task, or skip it |
| Late afternoon | Energy check | Rate your energy 1–5. Adjust plans down, never up |
| Evening | Wind-down | Dim lights, one calming thing, prepare for sleep |
| Night | Sleep | Aim for bed, not for "productive evening" |

## Rules for gentle schedules

- **Never schedule more than 2–3 things per day.** Empty space is part of the plan.
- **Build in rest blocks first.** Rest is not what is left over.
- **Use "around" times.** "Around noon" means 11:30 to 1:00 counts.
- **If you miss a block, skip it.** Do not try to "catch up."
- **Rewrite it when it stops working.** Your schedule should serve you, not the other way around.

## If you cannot follow the schedule

That is data, not failure. It means the schedule was too ambitious for today's energy. Make tomorrow's version shorter.
