# One Task at a Time

When your energy is low, a to-do list can feel like a wall. The trick is to look at only one brick.

## The idea

Pick **one** task. Do it. Stop. That is enough for now.

You do not need to pick the "most important" task. You do not need to pick the "right" task. You just need to pick *a* task, any task, and do only that until it is done — or until you genuinely cannot continue.

## How to do it

1. **Write down one tiny task.** Make it so small it feels almost silly. Examples: "wash one mug," "put on socks," "open the blinds," "drink a glass of water."
2. **Do that one thing.** Do not think about what comes after. Do not open your phone to "check something quickly."
3. **Stop when it is done.** Notice that you did it. That is a completed task.
4. **Decide if you want one more.** If you have energy, pick another single task. If not, rest. Both outcomes are correct.

## ADHD-friendly tips

- **Body doubling helps.** Do your one task while someone else is in the room, on a video call, or even just nearby working quietly.
- **Use a timer, not a schedule.** Set 5 or 10 minutes. You can stop when it beeps.
- **Lower the bar until you can step over it.** "Fold the laundry" becomes "put one sock in the drawer." That counts.
- **If you forget what the one task was, that is okay.** Pick a new one. No penalty.

## What counts as a task

Anything that takes your energy counts. Resting counts. Eating counts. Saying "no" counts. You are allowed to make your one task for the day "lie down and breathe."
